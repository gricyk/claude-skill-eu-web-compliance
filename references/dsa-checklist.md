# Digital Services Act checklist (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065)

The DSA applies in tiers. Most obligations scale with what kind of "intermediary service" a site is,
and many platform-specific duties only bind large platforms or exempt micro/small enterprises. Check
applicability before asserting a duty, do not apply VLOP-level obligations to a small community site.

## 1. Does the DSA apply to this site at all

The DSA covers "intermediary services": mere conduit, caching, and hosting services, including online
platforms that store and disseminate content to the public (e.g. comment sections, forums, marketplaces,
user-uploaded content). A purely static informational site with no user-submitted content that gets
published or stored for others to see is generally outside the DSA's hosting/platform obligations,
note this and move on. If there is a comment section, forum, file/photo upload gallery, classifieds, or
marketplace feature, the hosting-service rules below apply.

## 2. Size matters: which exemptions exist

Micro and small enterprises (as defined in Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, i.e. fewer than 50
staff and turnover or balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 10 million, `VERIFY` whether the operator
qualifies, including linked/partner enterprises) get two relevant exemptions:

- Art. 15(2): no annual content-moderation transparency report.
- Art. 19: the whole "online platform" section (Art. 20 to 28) does not apply, except Art. 24(3), unless
  the platform is designated a VLOP. So sections 5 to 8 below usually do **not** bind a small church,
  community, or small-business site, say so explicitly rather than listing them as duties.

There is **no** size exemption from the obligations for all intermediary and hosting services in
sections 3 and 4 (Art. 11 to 14, 16 to 18). Most small community or hobby sites also have no DSA
reporting duties toward the Commission, only toward their own users and national authorities.

## 3. Obligations for all intermediary services (Art. 11 to 14)

- A single point of contact for authorities (Art. 11) and for recipients of the service (Art. 12),
  published and easily accessible.
- A legal representative in the EU if the provider is established outside the EU but offers services
  in the EU (Art. 13).
- Terms and conditions must be clear, in plain and unambiguous language, and disclose any restrictions
  on use and any content moderation, including algorithmic decision-making and human review, and the
  internal complaint handling (Art. 14).

## 4. Hosting services: notice-and-action (Art. 16 to 18)

If the site hosts user-submitted content (comments, uploads), there must be an easy-to-access,
user-friendly, electronic mechanism for anyone to report content they believe is illegal (Art. 16). If
content is removed, restricted, or the account suspended, the affected user must receive a clear
statement of reasons (Art. 17). If the operator becomes aware of information giving rise to a suspicion
of a criminal offence involving a threat to life or safety, it must inform law enforcement (Art. 18).
These apply regardless of company size.

Sections 5 to 8 apply only to **online platforms** (hosting services that disseminate content to the
public) that are not micro or small enterprises, see section 2.

## 5. No dark patterns (Art. 25)

Online platform interfaces must not be designed to deceive, manipulate, or otherwise materially distort
users' ability to make free and informed decisions, e.g. making "decline" visually harder to find than
"accept", repeated nagging after a choice was already made, or disguised ads. This overlaps with, and
reinforces, the cookie-consent checks in `eprivacy-checklist.md`. Even where Art. 25 does not bind a
small operator, deceptive consent design is still a GDPR/ePrivacy consent-validity problem.

## 6. Advertising transparency (Art. 26)

If the site is an online platform displaying ads, users must be able to identify, for each ad, that it
is an ad, on whose behalf it is displayed, and who paid for it. Check any third-party ad placements for
this.

## 7. Recommender system transparency (Art. 27)

If the site has a recommender/personalization feature (e.g. "you might also like", personalized feed),
online platforms must explain in plain language the main parameters used and any options to influence
them.

## 8. Minors (Art. 28)

Online platforms accessible to minors must take appropriate measures for their privacy, safety, and
security, and must not present targeted advertising based on profiling using minors' data. Directly
relevant to any children's-group or youth-oriented site with profiles, accounts, or ad-supported pages
that does not qualify for the Art. 19 exemption. The Commission has issued guidelines under Art. 28(4),
`VERIFY` their current version.
